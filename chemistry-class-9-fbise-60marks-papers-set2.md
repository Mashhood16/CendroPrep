## Section B Solutions

### Q1 Solution (3 Marks) [SLO: C-09-B-04]
* **Physical Change:** This is a temporary, easily reversible change in which the physical state of matter changes but no new chemical substance is formed. Examples include the melting of ice and sublimation of ammonium chloride.
* **Chemical Change:** This is a permanent, irreversible change in which the chemical composition of matter changes, forming entirely new substances with different properties. Examples include the rusting of iron and burning of coal.

### Q2 Solution (3 Marks) [SLO: C-09-B-45]
* **Empirical Formula:** This is the simplest, whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound. For butane, the empirical formula is $C_2H_5$ (simplest ratio of 4:10).
* **Molecular Formula:** This is the actual number of atoms of each element present in one molecule of a compound. For butane, the molecular formula is $C_4H_{10}$.
* **Structural Formula:** This represents the actual arrangement and connectivity of atoms within the molecule. For butane, it is:

### Q1 Solution

#### **Haber's Process Dynamic Equilibrium:**
* **Dynamic Equilibrium Concept:** Reversible reactions in a closed system eventually reach a state of dynamic equilibrium where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction, and the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant.
* **Chemical Equation & Kc Expression:**
  $$N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g) \quad \Delta H = -92\text{ kJ/mol (Exothermic)}$$
  $$K_c = \frac{[NH_3]^2}{[N_2][H_2]^3}$$
* **Le Chatelier's - Pressure Effect:** The forward reaction is accompanied by a decrease in volume (4 moles of gaseous reactants yield 2 moles of gaseous product). Increasing the pressure shifts the equilibrium in the direction of fewer moles (forward), **substantially increasing the yield of ammonia ($NH_3$)**.
* **Le Chatelier's - Temperature Effect:** Since the reaction is highly exothermic, raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium backward (in the endothermic direction), **decreasing the yield of ammonia**. Therefore, industrial Haber's process uses an optimized moderate temperature of approximately $450^\circ C$ with an iron catalyst.

#### **Equilibrium Constant (Kc) Numerical Calculation:**
* **Problem Solving Strategy:** Write the balanced equilibrium equation and its Kc expression. Substitute the given equilibrium concentration values into the Kc expression. Solve mathematically and determine the final value with units.
* **Calculation:**
  Given: $[N_2] = 0.1\text{ M}$, $[H_2] = 0.3\text{ M}$, $[NH_3] = 0.2\text{ M}$
  $$K_c = \frac{[NH_3]^2}{[N_2][H_2]^3} = \frac{(0.2)^2}{(0.1) \times (0.3)^3}$$
  $$K_c = \frac{0.04}{0.1 \times 0.027} = \frac{0.04}{0.0027} = 14.81$$
* **Kc Units Determination:**
  $$\text{Units} = \frac{[\text{mol/dm}^3]^2}{[\text{mol/dm}^3] \times [\text{mol/dm}^3]^3} = \frac{1}{[\text{mol/dm}^3]^2} = \text{dm}^6\text{mol}^{-2}$$
* **Final Value:** $K_c = 14.81\text{ dm}^6\text{mol}^{-2}$.

### Q2 Solution

#### **Chemical Causes of Water Hardness:**
* **Temporary Hardness:** Caused by the presence of dissolved hydrogen carbonates (bicarbonates) of calcium and magnesium:
  $$\text{Calcium bicarbonate: } Ca(HCO_3)_2 \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Magnesium bicarbonate: } Mg(HCO_3)_2$$
* **Permanent Hardness:** Caused by the presence of dissolved sulfates and chlorides of calcium and magnesium:
  $$CaSO_4, MgSO_4, CaCl_2, MgCl_2$$
* **Removal of Temporary Hardness by Boiling:** Boiling decomposes soluble bicarbonates into insoluble carbonates, which precipitate out and can be filtered:
  $$Ca(HCO_3)_2(aq) \xrightarrow{\Delta} CaCO_3(s)\downarrow + H_2O(l) + CO_2(g)\uparrow$$
* **Removal of Temporary Hardness by Clark's Method:** Involves adding calculated slaked lime [$Ca(OH)_2$], which reacts with soluble bicarbonates to form insoluble carbonates:
  $$Ca(HCO_3)_2(aq) + Ca(OH)_2(aq) \longrightarrow 2CaCO_3(s)\downarrow + 2H_2O(l)$$
  $$Mg(HCO_3)_2(aq) + 2Ca(OH)_2(aq) \longrightarrow Mg(OH)_2(s)\downarrow + 2CaCO_3(s)\downarrow + 2H_2O(l)$$

#### **Permanent Hardness Removal via Sodium Zeolite Process:**
* **Ion Exchange Resins Concept:** Permanent hardness cannot be removed by boiling. Instead, it is treated using ion-exchange materials or resins.
* **Sodium Zeolite (Na2Z):** Sodium Zeolite is a natural or synthetic hydrated sodium aluminum silicate ($Na_2Z$).
* **Ion Exchange Process:** Hard water containing $Ca^{2+}$ and $Mg^{2+}$ ions is passed through a column filled with sodium zeolite. An exchange occurs where sodium ions ($Na^+$) from the zeolite are released into water, while $Ca^{2+}$ and $Mg^{2+}$ ions are trapped in the zeolite matrix.
* **Chemical Reactions:**
  $$Na_2Z(s) + Ca^{2+}(aq) \longrightarrow CaZ(s) + 2Na^+(aq)$$
  $$Na_2Z(s) + Mg^{2+}(aq) \longrightarrow MgZ(s) + 2Na^+(aq)$$
* **Regeneration of Zeolite:** When the zeolite becomes exhausted (fully converted to $CaZ$ or $MgZ$), it is regenerated by washing it with a concentrated brine (NaCl) solution, reclaiming its sodium form:
  $$CaZ(s) + 2NaCl(aq) \longrightarrow Na_2Z(s) + CaCl_2(aq)$$
* **Advantage:** The water becomes soft, containing harmless $Na^+$ ions, preventing boiler scales and soap wastage.

### Q1 Solution

#### **Acid-Base Theories & Lewis Coordinate Complex:**
* **Arrhenius Theory:** Acids produce hydrogen ions ($H^+$) in water; bases produce hydroxide ions ($OH^-$) in water.
* **Brønsted-Lowry Theory:** Acids are proton ($H^+$) donors; bases are proton ($H^+$) acceptors.
* **Lewis Theory:** Acids are electron-pair acceptors; bases are electron-pair donors.
* **Lewis Coordination Complex Formation ($NH_3$ and $BF_3$):**
  * **Ammonia ($NH_3$):** Central Nitrogen atom has 5 valence electrons, forms 3 covalent bonds with hydrogen, and retains one **lone electron pair** (Lewis Base).
  * **Boron Trifluoride ($BF_3$):** Central Boron atom has 3 valence electrons, forms 3 covalent bonds with fluorine, and contains an incomplete octet with a vacant $2p$ orbital (Lewis Acid).
  * **Coordinate Bond:** Nitrogen in $NH_3$ donates its lone pair into the vacant orbital of Boron in $BF_3$, forming a coordinate covalent bond represented by an arrow pointing from Donor to Acceptor:
    $$H_3N: + BF_3 \longrightarrow H_3N \longrightarrow BF_3 \quad \text{(Adduct Complex)}$$

#### **pH, pOH, and [OH-] Calculations:**
* **Problem Solving Strategy:** Write down the balanced dissociation equation of the strong diprotic acid $H_2SO_4$. Determine the concentration of $H^+$ ions by multiplying the acid molarity by 2. Use the pH formula ($pH = -\log[H^+]$). Calculate pOH using $pH + pOH = 14$. Use pOH to find $[OH^-]$ using $[OH^-] = 10^{-pOH}$.
* **Dissociation:**
  $$H_2SO_4(aq) \longrightarrow 2H^+(aq) + SO_4^{2-}(aq)$$
  $$[H^+] = 2 \times \text{Acid Concentration} = 2 \times 0.05\text{ M} = 0.1\text{ M} = 1.0 \times 10^{-1}\text{ M}$$
* **pH Calculation:**
  $$pH = -\log[H^+] = -\log(0.1) = 1.0$$
* **pOH Calculation:**
  $$pOH = 14.0 - pH = 14.0 - 1.0 = 13.0$$
* **Hydroxide Ion Concentration ($[OH^-]$):**
  $$[OH^-] = 10^{-pOH} = 1.0 \times 10^{-13}\text{ M}$$
* **Final Results:** $pH = 1.0$, $pOH = 13.0$, and $[OH^-] = 1.0 \times 10^{-13}\text{ M}$.

### Q2 Solution

#### **Ozone Chemistry & Stratospheric CFC Depletion:**
* **Chemical Composition:** Stratospheric ozone ($O_3$) is a triatomic molecule of oxygen formed by the reaction of molecular oxygen ($O_2$) with atomic oxygen ($O$) in the presence of solar ultraviolet light:
  $$O_2 + O \longrightarrow O_3$$
* **CFC Role:** Chlorofluorocarbons ($CF_2Cl_2$) are highly stable synthetic compounds. When they diffuse into the stratosphere, intense solar UV radiation decomposes them, releasing highly reactive **chlorine free radicals ($Cl^\bullet$)**:
  $$CF_2Cl_2(g) \xrightarrow{h\nu} CF_2Cl^\bullet(g) + Cl^\bullet(g)$$
* **Catalytic Destruction Mechanism:** A single chlorine radical reacts with ozone to form a chlorine monoxide radical ($ClO^\bullet$) and molecular oxygen. The $ClO^\bullet$ subsequently reacts with atomic oxygen to regenerate the chlorine radical, creating a catalytic cycle:
  $$Cl^\bullet(g) + O_3(g) \longrightarrow ClO^\bullet(g) + O_2(g)$$
  $$ClO^\bullet(g) + O(g) \longrightarrow Cl^\bullet(g) + O_2(g)$$
  $$\text{Net Reaction: } O_3(g) + O(g) \longrightarrow 2O_2(g)$$
* **Impact:** Because the chlorine radical is regenerated, a single chlorine atom can catalytically destroy up to **100,000 ozone molecules** before being diffused out.

#### **Environmental Impacts & Mitigation Strategies:**
* **UV Exposure:** Depletion of stratospheric ozone allows high-energy **ultraviolet-B (UV-B) radiation** to reach the Earth's surface.
* **Human Health Consequences:** UV-B breaks covalent bonds in biomolecules, leading to mutations in DNA, causing skin cancers, cataract formation in eyes, and suppression of the human immune system.
* **Ecological Consequences:** UV-B inhibits photosynthesis in phytoplankton (the base of marine food webs) and decreases crop yields (e.g., rice, soybeans).
* **Mitigation Strategies:**
  1. **Phase-out of ODS (Montreal Protocol):** Complete global ban on the production and use of chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and halons.
  2. **Replacement with HFCs:** Transitioning to ozone-friendly alternatives like hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) or hydrofluoroolefins (HFOs) which decompose easily in the troposphere without releasing chlorine radicals.

### Q1 Solution

#### **Hydrocarbon Classifications & Addition Reactions:**
* **Alkanes:** Saturated hydrocarbons containing only single carbon-carbon covalent bonds ($C-C$). They are relatively stable and undergo substitution reactions. General formula: $C_nH_{2n+2}$.
* **Alkenes:** Unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-to-carbon double bond ($C=C$). They are highly reactive. General formula: $C_nH_{2n}$.
* **Alkynes:** Unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-to-carbon triple bond ($C \equiv C$). They are also highly reactive. General formula: $C_nH_{2n-2}$.
* **Addition Reactions of Ethene ($C_2H_4$):**
  * **Hydrogenation:** Ethene reacts with hydrogen gas in the presence of a nickel catalyst at $250^\circ C$ to yield saturated ethane:
    $$CH_2=CH_2(g) + H_2(g) \xrightarrow[\Delta]{\text{Ni}} CH_3-CH_3(g)$$
  * **Bromination (Halogenation):** Ethene reacts with bromine water to form 1,2-dibromoethane:
    $$CH_2=CH_2(g) + Br_2(aq) \longrightarrow CH_2Br-CH_2Br(l)$$
  * **Hydration:** Ethene reacts with steam at $300^\circ C$ and high pressure in the presence of phosphoric acid ($H_3PO_4$) catalyst to yield ethanol:
    $$CH_2=CH_2(g) + H_2O(g) \xrightarrow[\text{Pressure}]{\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4} CH_3-CH_2-OH(l)$$

#### **Ethane vs. Ethene Saturated Chemical Test:**
* **Test Reagent:** **Bromine Water ($Br_2/H_2O$)** is used. It is a reddish-brown liquid.
* **Ethene Behavior (Unsaturated):** When ethene gas is bubbled through bromine water, an addition reaction occurs, converting bromine into 1,2-dibromoethane (colorless). **The reddish-brown color of bromine water is instantly discharged (decolorized)**.
* **Ethane Behavior (Saturated):** Alkanes like ethane do not react with bromine water under normal laboratory conditions due to the lack of reactive double bonds. **The reddish-brown color of the bromine solution persists completely**.
* **Chemical Significance:** This reaction represents a standard qualitative test used by analytical chemists to verify the presence of carbon-carbon unsaturation in organic compounds.

### Q2 Solution

#### **Electroplating Copper onto an Iron Key:**
* **Electrolytic Cell Setup:** The process of electroplating involves depositing a thin layer of a superior metal (copper) onto an inferior metal (iron) using electrical current.
* **Anode (Oxidation Electrode):** A sheet of pure copper metal is connected to the positive terminal of the DC battery.
* **Cathode (Reduction Electrode):** The iron key to be electroplated is cleaned thoroughly with acid and connected to the negative terminal of the battery.
* **Electrolyte Solution:** An aqueous solution of a soluble copper salt, typically **Copper Sulfate ($CuSO_4(aq)$)**, is used.
* **Physical Process:** When current passes through the cell, copper atoms from the anode oxidize and dissolve as copper ions ($Cu^{2+}$) into the electrolyte. Concurrently, $Cu^{2+}$ ions from the solution migrate to the negative cathode (iron key), where they gain electrons and deposit as a uniform, thin, shiny layer of red-brown copper metal.

#### **Electrochemical Electrode Reactions & Corrosion Prevention:**
* **Anode Half-Reaction (Oxidation):** Copper atoms lose electrons and dissolve:
  $$Cu(s) \longrightarrow Cu^{2+}(aq) + 2e^-$$
* **Cathode Half-Reaction (Reduction):** Copper ions gain electrons and deposit on the iron key:
  $$Cu^{2+}(aq) + 2e^- \longrightarrow Cu(s) \quad \text{(Deposits on Iron key)}$$
* **Concentration Stability:** Since the rate of copper oxidation at the anode equals the rate of copper deposition at the cathode, the concentration of $Cu^{2+}$ in the electrolyte remains completely constant.
* **Corrosion Prevention Mechanism:** The deposited layer of copper acts as a physical barrier. Copper is far less reactive than iron and does not readily oxidize in atmospheric moisture. By sealing the iron key away from water and oxygen, it prevents the electrochemical process of rusting.

## Section C Solutions

### Q1 Solution

#### **Nature of Hydrogen Bonding & Water's Density Anomaly:**
* **Nature of Hydrogen Bonding:** Hydrogen bonding is a strong electrostatic intermolecular attraction that occurs when a hydrogen atom covalently bonded to a highly electronegative, small atom (like fluorine, oxygen, or nitrogen) experiences the positive pole ($H^{\delta+}$) and attracts a lone pair on a neighboring electronegative atom ($O^{\delta-}$, $N^{\delta-}$, or $F^{\delta-}$).
* **Anomalous Density of Water:** Most liquids contract and become denser as they cool. Water contracts and increases in density as it cools, but only until it reaches **$4^\circ C$** (maximum density of $1.0\text{ g/cm}^3$).
* **Structural Behavior below $4^\circ C$:** As the temperature drops below $4^\circ C$, water molecules slow down, and hydrogen bonds begin to orient them into a rigid, highly organized hexagonal lattice.
* **Open Cage Structure of Ice:** This lattice forces water molecules to push farther apart into an **open, cage-like arrangement**, increasing the volume of the solid phase. Because the volume increases, **ice has a lower density than liquid water at $0^\circ C$** ($0.917\text{ g/cm}^3$ vs $1.0\text{ g/cm}^3$), causing ice to float. This anomaly is crucial for aquatic life, as floating ice insulates lakes.

#### **Boiling Point Comparisons (HF, H2O, NH3):**
* **Intermolecular Attractions Comparison:** The boiling points of these compounds are abnormally high compared to other hydrides of their groups, which is attributed to hydrogen bonding.
* **Water ($H_2O$ Boiling Point = $100^\circ C$):** Water has a much higher boiling point than $HF$ or $NH_3$ because each water molecule can form an average of **four hydrogen bonds** per molecule, creating a massive 3D intermolecular network.
* **Hydrogen Fluoride ($HF$ Boiling Point = $19.5^\circ C$):** Fluorine is more electronegative than oxygen, forming stronger individual hydrogen bonds. However, each $HF$ molecule can only form an average of **two hydrogen bonds** (in a zig-zag chain), so it has a lower boiling point than water.
* **Ammonia ($NH_3$ Boiling Point = $-33^\circ C$):** Nitrogen is less electronegative than oxygen or fluorine, so its hydrogen bonds are weaker. Each molecule can only participate in an average of **two hydrogen bonds**, resulting in the lowest boiling point and highest volatility of the three.

### Q2 Solution

#### **Classification and Biological Roles of Carbohydrates:**
* **Monosaccharides (Simple Sugars):**
  * **Properties:** They are the simplest sugars, sweet, soluble in water, and cannot be hydrolyzed into simpler units. General formula: $(CH_2O)_n$.
  * **Examples & Roles:** **Glucose** (blood sugar, primary cellular energy source) and **Fructose** (fruit sugar).
* **Oligosaccharides (Complex Sugars):**
  * **Properties:** Composed of 2 to 10 monosaccharide units joined covalently. On hydrolysis, they yield simple sugars.
  * **Examples & Roles:** **Sucrose** (table sugar, transport form of carbohydrates in plants) and **Lactose** (milk sugar, energy source for mammalian infants).
* **Polysaccharides (Biochemical Polymers):**
  * **Properties:** Amorphous, tasteless, insoluble high-molecular-weight polymers composed of hundreds of monosaccharide units.
  * **Examples & Roles:** **Starch** (energy storage in plants), **Glycogen** (energy storage in animal liver and muscles), and **Cellulose** (builds plant cell walls).

#### **Glycosidic Linkage Condensation Synthesis of Sucrose:**
* **Condensation Polymerization:** Disaccharides are synthesized when two monosaccharide monomers undergo a chemical condensation reaction.
* **Sucrose Synthesis:** Sucrose ($C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}$) is synthesized by the condensation reaction between a molecule of **$\alpha$-D-glucose** and a molecule of **$\beta$-D-fructose**.
* **Water Elimination:** The reaction involves the hydroxyl group ($-OH$) of carbon-1 on glucose combining with the hydroxyl group ($-OH$) of carbon-2 on fructose. This reaction eliminates a water molecule ($H_2O$):
  $$C_6H_{12}O_6\text{ (Glucose)} + C_6H_{12}O_6\text{ (Fructose)} \longrightarrow C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}\text{ (Sucrose)} + H_2O\text{ (Water)}$$
* **Glycosidic Linkage:** The remaining oxygen atom bridges the two ring structures, forming a stable covalent **C-O-C glycosidic bond**.

### Q1 Solution (3 Marks) [SLO: C-09-H-01]
* **Reversible Reactions:** These are reactions in which the products can recombine to form the original reactants under identical conditions. They never go to completion and are written with a double arrow ($\rightleftharpoons$). Example:
  $$N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g)$$
* **Irreversible Reactions:** These are reactions in which the products cannot recombine to reform the original reactants. They proceed to completion and are written with a single arrow ($\longrightarrow$). Example:
  $$2H_2(g) + O_2(g) \longrightarrow 2H_2O(l)$$

### Q3 Solution (3 Marks) [SLO: C-09-F-03]

**Problem Solving Strategy:** Calculate the molar mass of carbon dioxide ($CO_2 = 44\text{ g/mol}$). Determine the number of moles in 11g of $CO_2$. Use the molar volume relation at STP ($1.0\text{ mole} = 22.4\text{ dm}^3$) to calculate the volume occupied by the calculated moles.

**Solution:**
1. Molar Mass of $CO_2$ = $12 + (2 \times 16) = 44\text{ g/mol}$
2. Calculate Moles:
   $$\text{Moles of } CO_2 = \frac{\text{Given Mass}}{\text{Molar Mass}} = \frac{11}{44} = 0.25\text{ moles}$$
3. Calculate Volume occupied at STP:
   $$\text{Volume} = \text{Moles} \times \text{Molar Volume} = 0.25 \times 22.4 = 5.6\text{ dm}^3$$

### Q6 Solution (3 Marks) [SLO: C-09-J-05]

**Problem Solving Strategy:** Potassium hydroxide ($KOH$) is a strong univalent base that dissociates completely in water. Determine the concentration of hydroxide ions ($[OH^-]$) from $KOH$ molarity. Use $[OH^-]$ to calculate pOH ($pOH = -\log[OH^-]$). Subtract pOH from 14 to determine the pH ($pH = 14 - pOH$).

**Solution:**
1. Dissociation of KOH:
   $$KOH(aq) \longrightarrow K^+(aq) + OH^-(aq)$$
2. Hydroxide ion concentration $[OH^-]$ = $1 \times 10^{-3}\text{ M}$
3. Calculate pOH:
   $$pOH = -\log[OH^-] = -\log(1 \times 10^{-3}) = 3.0$$
4. Calculate pH:
   $$pH = 14.0 - pOH = 14.0 - 3.0 = 11.0$$
5. Result: The pH of the $KOH$ solution is 11.0, representing a basic solution.

### Q1 Solution

#### **Periodic Trends: Ionization Energy & Electron Affinity:**
* **Ionization Energy (IE):** The minimum energy required to remove the outermost valence electron from an isolated gaseous neutral atom.
* **Electron Affinity (EA):** The change in enthalpy when an electron is added to an isolated gaseous neutral atom to form a univalent negative anion.
* **Period Trend (Left to Right):** Both **IE and EA generally increase** across a period. This is because:
  * **Nuclear Pull:** Protons are added to the nucleus, increasing the **effective nuclear charge ($Z_{eff}$)**.
  * **Atomic Size:** Valence electrons are pulled closer to the nucleus, decreasing atomic size.
  * **Shielding Effect:** Remains relatively constant across a period as electrons are added to the same energy shell.
  * **Result:** Outer-shell electrons are held more tightly, requiring more energy to remove (higher IE) and strongly attracting incoming electrons (higher EA).
* **Group Trend (Top to Bottom):** Both **IE and EA generally decrease** down a group. This is because:
  * **New Shells:** Each step down adds a completely new principal quantum energy shell, substantially increasing atomic size.
  * **Shielding Effect:** The number of inner shell electrons increases, shielding the outer valence electrons from nuclear attraction.
  * **Result:** The valence shell experiences a weaker nuclear hold, making it easier to remove outer electrons (lower IE) and reducing nuclear attraction for incoming electrons (lower EA).

#### **First Ionization Energy Anomaly: Nitrogen vs. Oxygen:**
* **Electronic Configurations:**
  * **Nitrogen ($Z=7$):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p_x^1 2p_y^1 2p_z^1$ (half-filled $2p$-subshell).
  * **Oxygen ($Z=8$):** $1s^2 2s^2 2p_x^2 2p_y^1 2p_z^1$ (partially filled $2p$-subshell).
* **Half-Filled Subshell Stability:** In quantum mechanics, **half-filled ($p^3$) and completely filled ($p^6$) subshells possess exceptional thermodynamic stability** due to symmetric electron distribution and exchange energy.
* **Nitrogen Stability:** Nitrogen's $2p$-subshell is exactly half-filled ($2p^3$), with one electron in each of the three $p$ orbitals. This stable configuration resists the removal of an electron, requiring an anomalously high first ionization energy ($1402\text{ kJ/mol}$).
* **Oxygen Instability:** Oxygen has four electrons in its $2p$-subshell ($2p^4$). The $2p_x$ orbital contains two paired electrons. The electron-electron repulsion between these paired electrons in the same orbital makes it significantly easier to remove one electron, resulting in a lower first ionization energy ($1314\text{ kJ/mol}$) despite its larger nuclear charge.

### Q2 Solution

#### **Triglycerides & Saturated vs. Unsaturated Fatty Acids:**
* **Chemical Structure of Triglycerides:** Triglycerides are biological esters synthesized by the condensation of one molecule of **glycerol** (a trihydroxy alcohol) with three molecules of **fatty acids** (long-chain carboxylic acids). This reaction eliminates three water molecules:
  $$\text{Glycerol} + 3\text{ Fatty Acids} \longrightarrow \text{Triglyceride} + 3H_2O$$
* **Saturated Fatty Acids:**
  * **Bonding:** Contain only single covalent carbon-carbon bonds ($C-C$) in their hydrocarbon chains.
  * **Melting Points:** Their straight chains pack closely, resulting in strong intermolecular forces and high melting points. They are **solids** at room temperature (known as fats, e.g., butter, animal fat).
  * **Health Impact:** Consuming excess saturated fats increases low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol, leading to plaque build-up in arteries (atherosclerosis) and coronary heart disease.
* **Unsaturated Fatty Acids:**
  * **Bonding:** Contain one or more double covalent carbon-carbon bonds ($C=C$) in their hydrocarbon chains, creating bends (kinks) in their structure.
  * **Melting Points:** The kinks prevent close packing, resulting in weaker intermolecular attractions and low melting points. They are **liquids** at room temperature (known as oils, e.g., olive oil, sunflower oil).
  * **Health Impact:** Unsaturated fatty acids, particularly polyunsaturated ones, are healthier, helping reduce LDL cholesterol and supporting cardiovascular health.

#### **Vitamin Classifications, Roles, and Deficiency Diseases:**
* **Fat-Soluble vs. Water-Soluble Vitamins:**
  * **Fat-Soluble (A, D, E, K):** Soluble in lipids and absorbed with dietary fats. They are **stored in the liver and adipose tissues** and are not excreted easily, meaning excess intake can cause toxicity (hypervitaminosis).
  * **Water-Soluble (B-complex, C):** Soluble in water and not stored in the body in significant amounts. Excess is **excreted in urine**, so they must be supplied regularly in our daily diet.
* **Vitamin A (Fat-Soluble):**
  * **Biological Role:** Essential for the synthesis of rhodopsin (visual purple) in the retina, supporting low-light vision and cellular health.
  * **Sources & Deficiency:** Found in carrots, spinach, liver, and dairy. Deficiency causes **night blindness** and xerophthalmia.
* **Vitamin C (Water-Soluble, Ascorbic Acid):**
  * **Biological Role:** Required for collagen synthesis (connective tissue repair), wound healing, and immune support.
  * **Sources & Deficiency:** Found in citrus fruits (oranges, lemons) and tomatoes. Deficiency causes **scurvy** (bleeding gums, joint pain, loose teeth).
* **Vitamin D (Fat-Soluble):**
  * **Biological Role:** Regulates the absorption of calcium and phosphorus from the gut to support bone mineralization.
  * **Sources & Deficiency:** Synthesized in skin exposed to sunlight; found in fish oil and eggs. Deficiency in children causes **rickets** (deformed, soft bones) and osteomalacia in adults.
