### Question 3 Solution

(a) Analyze the three main allotropes of carbon: Diamond, Graphite, and Buckyballs. Differentiate them based on hybridization/structure, conductivity, and hardness. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Allotropy Definition:** The phenomenon in which an element exists in more than one physical form having different physical properties but identical chemical properties [96].
- **Diamond Structure:** Each carbon atom is $sp^3$ hybridized and bonded to four other carbon atoms in a rigid, three-dimensional tetrahedral lattice [97]. The C-C bond length is $1.544 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m}$ with bond angles of $109.5^\circ$ [97].
- **Graphite Structure:** Carbon atoms are $sp^2$ hybridized, where each atom is bonded to three others within flat, two-dimensional hexagonal layers [96, 98]. The adjacent layers are separated by a distance of $3.35 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m}$ and held by weak Van der Waals forces [96].
- **Buckyballs (C-60) Structure:** A hollow spherical cage composed of 60 carbon atoms arranged in pentagons and hexagons, resembling a soccer ball [97].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
- **Hardness:**
  - *Diamond:* Extremely hard [97]. This is because of its rigid, interlocking three-dimensional network of strong covalent bonds [97].
  - *Graphite:* Soft and slippery [96]. The hexagonal sheets slide over one another due to weak interlayer forces, making it an excellent dry lubricant [96].
  - *Buckyballs:* Hard but highly resilient, absorbing extreme impacts reversibly [97].
- **Electrical Conductivity:**
  - *Diamond:* Insulator [97]. All valence electrons are tightly bound in localized $sp^3$ bonds, leaving no mobile electrons [97].
  - *Graphite:* Excellent conductor [96]. Each carbon has a free delocalized electron on its unhybridized $2p_z$ orbital, which can migrate along the sheets [96].
  - *Buckyballs:* Semiconductors in pure form [97].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
The molecular structures are confirmed by crystal analyses and can be represented visually:
- **Diamond Tetrahedral Lattice:** Draw carbon atoms as spheres connected by thick lines to four others at $109.5^\circ$ angles, forming a continuous 3D network of tetrahedral cells [97, 100].
- **Graphite Layered Lattice:** Draw two parallel planes consisting of repeating hexagonal rings [96, 100]. Vertical dashed lines are drawn between the planes to show the weak interlayer cohesive forces [96, 100].
- **Buckyball Spherical Cage:** Draw a closed sphere composed of interlocking 20 hexagons and 12 pentagons with a carbon atom at each of the 60 vertices [97, 100].

#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Definition of Matter:** Any physical entity that has mass and occupies space by having volume [101].
- **Classification Principle:** Matter is divided into Pure Substances (constant chemical composition and properties) and Mixtures (physical blend of two or more substances in variable proportions) [101].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Sub-divisions of Pure Substances:**

  - *Heterogeneous:* Non-uniform mixtures with distinct boundaries, including:

### Question 4 Solution

(a) Describe the preparation and testing of Saturated, Unsaturated, and Supersaturated solutions using sugar and water as an experimental example. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Solution Classification:** Solutions are classified by their solute content relative to the solvent's maximum capacity at a given temperature [102]:
  - *Unsaturated:* Can dissolve more solute [102].
  - *Saturated:* Holds the maximum solute; in dynamic equilibrium [102].
  - *Supersaturated:* Unstable system holding excess solute beyond saturation [102].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
- **Experimental Preparation:**
  1. *Unsaturated:* Add 10g of sugar into 100g of water at $20^\circ\text{C}$ and stir [102]. The sugar dissolves completely, and because more can dissolve, the solution is unsaturated [102].
  2. *Saturated:* Continue adding sugar with stirring at $20^\circ\text{C}$ until no more dissolves [102]. Saturated state is achieved when solid sugar begins settling at the bottom of the beaker [102].
  3. *Supersaturated:* Heat the saturated mixture with excess sugar to $60^\circ\text{C}$ [102]. All excess sugar dissolves due to increased thermal solubility [102]. Cool this solution slowly and undisturbed back to $20^\circ\text{C}$ [102]. This clear solution is now supersaturated [102].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **The Seed Crystal Test:** To identify each solution, drop a single crystal of sugar into each beaker:
  - *Unsaturated Beaker:* The added crystal dissolves quickly because the solvent has remaining capacity [102].
  - *Saturated Beaker:* The crystal sinks and remains unchanged, as dissolution and crystallization rates are equal [102].
  - *Supersaturated Beaker:* The crystal acts as a seed, triggering rapid crystallization [102]. Excess solute precipitates out instantly, leaving a stable saturated solution behind [102].
- **Experimental Diagram:** Draw three beakers labeled Unsaturated (clear with dissolved solute), Saturated (clear with a thin layer of excess solute at bottom), and Supersaturated (showing rapid crystallization spreading from a seed crystal) [102].

(b) Define Solubility. Analyze the effect of temperature on solubility curves, citing examples of solute types whose solubility increases, decreases, or remains constant. (4 Marks)
#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Solubility Definition:** The maximum mass of a solute in grams that can dissolve in 100 grams of a solvent at a specific temperature and pressure to form a saturated solution [103].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Thermodynamic Behavior:**
  - *Endothermic Dissolution ($\Delta H > 0$):* Solubility increases with temperature [103]. Heat absorption drives the equilibrium forward [103]. E.g., $\text{KNO}_3$ and $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ [104].
  - *Exothermic Dissolution ($\Delta H < 0$):* Solubility decreases with temperature [103]. E.g., $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ and gaseous $\text{SO}_2$ [104].
  - *Athermal Dissolution ($\Delta H \approx 0$):* Solubility remains nearly constant [103]. E.g., $\text{NaCl}$ [104].

#### Chemical Equations & Examples
- **Solubility Curves:** Graph plotting solubility ($\text{g}/100\text{g } \text{H}_2\text{O}$) against temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$) [103]:
  - *$\text{KNO}_3$ Curve:* Steeps sharply upwards, from $32\text{ g}$ at $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $246\text{ g}$ at $100^\circ\text{C}$ [103, 104].
  - *$\text{NaCl}$ Curve:* A nearly flat line, changing only from $35.7\text{ g}$ at $0^\circ\text{C}$ to $39.2\text{ g}$ at $100^\circ\text{C}$ [104].
  - *$\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ Curve:* Slopes downwards, from $0.173\text{ g}$ at $20^\circ\text{C}$ to $0.066\text{ g}$ at $100^\circ\text{C}$ [104].

(a) Compare Solutions, Colloids, and Suspensions in a comprehensive matrix based on particle size, homogeneity, Tyndall effect, filtration, and settling behavior. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Definitions:**
  - *Solution:* Homogeneous system with solute particles fully dissolved, size $< 1\text{ nm}$ [105].
  - *Colloid:* Heterogeneous system appearing homogeneous, particle size $1 - 1000\text{ nm}$ [105].
  - *Suspension:* Heterogeneous coarse mixture, particle size $> 1000\text{ nm}$ [105].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
- **Comparative Matrix:**

| Parameter | Solution | Colloid | Suspension |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Homogeneity** | Homogeneous [105]. | Heterogeneous-looking [105]. | Coarse heterogeneous [105]. |
| **Particle Size** | $< 1\text{ nm}$ [105]. | $1 - 1000\text{ nm}$ [105]. | $> 1000\text{ nm}$ [105]. |
| **Filtration** | Passes ordinary/ultra filters [105]. | Passes ordinary, blocked by ultra [105]. | Retained by ordinary paper [105]. |
| **Tyndall Effect** | No scattering [105]. | Strong scattering of light [105]. | Scatters or blocks light [105]. |
| **Stability** | Highly stable; no settling [105]. | Stable; no settling [105]. | Unstable; settles down [105]. |

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **The Tyndall Effect:** When a light beam passes through a true solution, the beam path is invisible because the small particles do not scatter light [105]. In a colloid, the path glows brightly (Tyndall cone) because intermediate particles scatter light waves [105].
- **Experimental Setup Diagram:** Draw a light source sending a laser beam through two beakers in sequence: Beaker 1 (Solution - beam path is invisible) and Beaker 2 (Colloid - beam path glows as a visible cone of light) [105].

### Question 3 Solution

(a) Describe Rutherford's Gold Foil Experiment. Detail the observations, conclusions, and defects identified in this model of the atom. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Rutherford Experiment (1911):** Performed to probe the internal structure of the atom [114].
- **Experimental Method:** Bombarded alpha ($\alpha$) particles emitted from polonium onto an extremely thin gold foil of $0.00004\text{ cm}$ thickness [114]. Alpha particles are doubly charged helium ions ($\text{He}^{2+}$) [114]. A fluorescent screen coated with zinc sulfide ($\text{ZnS}$) detected particle deflections [114].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
- **Observations & Conclusions:**
  1. *Observation:* Most alpha particles passed straight through the foil undeflected [114, 124].
     *Conclusion:* The majority of the volume within an atom is empty space [114, 124].
  2. *Observation:* A few particles deflected at very large angles [114, 124].
     *Conclusion:* There is a concentrated positive charge in the center of the atom, which repelled the positive alpha particles [114, 124]. Rutherford named this the **nucleus** [114].
  3. *Observation:* Very few particles (1 in 20,000) deflected back completely ($180^\circ$) [114, 124].
     *Conclusion:* The nucleus is extremely dense, containing almost the entire mass of the atom [114, 124].
- **Defects of Rutherford's Model:**
  1. *Electromagnetic Collapse:* Classical physics says revolving charged electrons must continuously emit radiation, making their orbit decay until they collapse into the nucleus [114, 115].
  2. *Continuous Spectrum:* Continuous energy loss should produce a continuous spectrum, but atoms display a discrete line spectrum [114, 115].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **Gold Foil Deflection Diagram:** Shows concentric circles of gold atoms with a tiny positive nucleus at the center of each [114]. Parallel arrows show alpha particles: most passing straight through, some near the center bending at wide angles, and one colliding head-on with the nucleus and rebounding [114].

(b) Describe Bohr's Atomic Theory in detail. State all of its core postulates and write the mathematical expression for energy differences. (4 Marks)
#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Bohr Atomic Theory (1913):** Formulated to resolve Rutherford's defects by incorporating Planck's quantum theory [114].
- **Core Principle:** Electrons do not radiate energy while moving within specific, non-radiating circular orbits known as stationary states [114, 115].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Key Postulates:**
  1. *Stationary Orbits:* Electrons revolve only in circular orbits with quantized radius and energy, designated by $n = 1, 2, 3...$ [114, 118].
  2. *Angular Momentum Quantization:* An electron revolves only in orbits where its angular momentum ($mvr$) is an integral multiple of $\frac{h}{2\pi}$:
     $$mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}$$
     where $m$ is electron mass, $v$ is velocity, $r$ is radius, and $h$ is Planck's constant ($6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J s}$) [114].
  3. *Energy Transitions:* Energy is emitted or absorbed only when an electron jumps between orbits [114].

#### Chemical Equations & Examples
When an electron absorbs energy, it jumps from $E_1$ to $E_2$. Upon returning, it emits a photon of identical energy:
$$\Delta E = E_2 - E_1 = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \quad [114]$$
This quantized packet of energy produces a line spectrum [114].
- **Bohr Model Diagram:** Shows circular concentric shells ($n=1, 2, 3$) surrounding a positive nucleus ($+$) [114]. An arrow shows an electron absorbing a photon to jump to an outer shell, and another arrow shows the electron emitting a photon to drop to an inner shell [114].

(a) Explain the concept of Metallic Bonding using the Electron Sea Model. Detail how this bonding accounts for electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, and malleability. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Metallic Bonding:** The chemical bond holding metal atoms together, described by the **Electron Sea Model** [113, 129].
- **Electron Sea Model:** Metals have low ionization energies and lose their valence electrons easily [129]. These valence electrons form a highly mobile, delocalized "sea" of electrons [129]. The remaining positive metal cations are locked in a rigid three-dimensional repeating lattice held together by the attractive sea [129].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
- **Explaining Metallic Properties:**
  - *Electrical Conductivity:* Applying an electrical voltage across a metal drives the mobile delocalized electrons to flow freely toward the positive terminal, creating current [129].
  - *Thermal Conductivity:* Mobile electrons at the heated end absorb heat energy, increasing their kinetic energy [129]. They move rapidly through the lattice and collide with other electrons and metal cations, transferring heat [129].
  - *Malleability:* When a hammer strikes a metal, the positive cation layers slide past each other [129]. The mobile electron sea shifts along with them, maintaining the attractive bond and preventing cleavage [129].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **Electron Sea Lattice Diagram:** Draw positive circles labeled "Metal Cations" arranged in perfect rows [129]. Surrounding them are numerous small dots labeled "Delocalized Electrons" with wavy arrows to indicate their free-flowing movement in all directions [129].
- **Metal Bending (Malleability) Diagram:** Shows a row of cations sliding under an applied force, while the surrounding electron dots adjust their positions continuously to prevent the metal from fracturing [129].

(b) Define Isotopes. Detail the isotopic structures, abundance, and relative atomic masses of Carbon and Chlorine. (4 Marks)
#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Isotope Definition:** Atoms of the exact same element with the same atomic number (protons) but different mass numbers (neutrons) [116]. They possess identical chemical properties but slightly different physical properties [116, 130].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Isotopic Structures & Abundances:**
  - *Carbon Isotopes ($Z=6$):*
    1. *Carbon-12 ($^{12}\text{C}$):* 6 protons, 6 neutrons, 6 electrons [130]. Highly stable with $98.8\%$ abundance [130].
    2. *Carbon-13 ($^{13}\text{C}$):* 6 protons, 7 neutrons, 6 electrons ($1.1\%$ abundance) [130].
    3. *Carbon-14 ($^{14}\text{C}$):* 6 protons, 8 neutrons, 6 electrons ($0.009\%$ trace abundance) [130].
  - *Chlorine Isotopes ($Z=17$):*
    1. *Chlorine-35 ($^{35}\text{Cl}$):* 17 protons, 18 neutrons, 17 electrons ($75.7\%$ abundance) [130].
    2. *Chlorine-37 ($^{37}\text{Cl}$):* 17 protons, 20 neutrons, 17 electrons ($24.23\%$ abundance) [130].

#### Chemical Equations & Examples
The fractional relative atomic mass is the weighted average of isotopic masses [130]:
$$\text{RAM of Cl} = \frac{(\text{Mass}_1 \times \%_1) + (\text{Mass}_2 \times \%_2)}{100}$$
$$\text{RAM of Cl} = \frac{(35 \times 75.7) + (37 \times 24.3)}{100} = \frac{2649.5 + 899.1}{100} = 35.5\text{ amu} \quad [130]$$
This weighted calculation explains why chlorine has a fractional atomic mass [130].

### Question 3 Solution

(a) Discuss Electrochemistry in detail. Explain the working and construction of a Galvanic (Voltaic) cell with a complete labeled diagram and half-cell equations. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Galvanic Cell Definition:** An electrochemical cell that spontaneously converts chemical energy into electrical energy using spontaneous oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions [137].
- **Cell Construction:**
  1. *Anode Half-cell:* A pure Zinc ($\text{Zn}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Zinc Sulfate ($\text{ZnSO}_4$) solution [147].
  2. *Cathode Half-cell:* A pure Copper ($\text{Cu}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Copper Sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$) solution [147].
  3. *Salt Bridge:* A U-shaped glass tube containing a concentrated gel of potassium chloride ($\text{KCl}$) to maintain electrical neutrality and complete the circuit [147].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
The cell runs on separate half-cell reactions:
- **At Zn Anode (Oxidation half-reaction):** Zinc acts as the negative electrode [138]. Zinc atoms lose two electrons to form Zinc ions which enter the solution [147]:
  $$\text{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \quad (E^\circ = +0.76\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **At Cu Cathode (Reduction half-reaction):** Electrons flow through the wire to the copper electrode [147]. Copper ions in the cathode solution absorb these electrons and deposit as copper [147]:
  $$\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ = +0.34\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **Overall Cell Redox Reaction:**
  $$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = +1.10\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
  This spontaneous reaction yields an electrical potential of $1.1\text{ V}$ [147].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **Galvanic Cell Labeled Diagram:**
  ```
               [ VOLTMETER (e- flow -->) ]
               /                         \
         Zn Anode(-)                     Cu Cathode(+)
         [1M ZnSO4]                      [1M CuSO4]
              \                             /
               \_____[  SALT BRIDGE  ]_____/
                      (K+ and Cl- ions)
  ```
  - *Salt Bridge Mechanism:* Zinc ions accumulate in the anode compartment, and sulfate ions accumulate in the cathode compartment [147]. Inert chloride ions ($\text{Cl}^-$) migrate from the salt bridge to the anode, while potassium ions ($\text{K}^+$) migrate to the cathode, neutralizing charges [147].

(b) Explain the electrolysis of molten sodium chloride in Down's cell. Provide diagrams, chemical equations, and the industrial significance of this process. (4 Marks)
#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Down's Cell Process:** An electrochemical system designed for the commercial-scale extraction of pure metallic sodium through the electrolysis of molten salt ($\text{NaCl}$) [148].
- **Core Principle:** Electric current forces a non-spontaneous redox reaction where sodium ions are reduced to sodium metal and chloride ions are oxidized to chlorine gas [148].

#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Periodic Properties:** Characteristics of elements showing periodic variations with increasing atomic numbers [149].
  - *Atomic Radius:* Half the distance between the nuclei of two bonded identical atoms [150].
  - *Ionization Energy (IE):* Energy required to remove the outermost electron from a gaseous atom [144].
  - *Electron Affinity (EA):* Energy released when an electron is added to a gaseous atom [150].
  - *Electronegativity (EN):* Relative pull on shared bonding electrons [137, 150].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
- **Trends Across a Period (Left to Right):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Decreases:* Protons are added to the nucleus, increasing the effective nuclear charge, pulling electron shells tighter [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Increase:* Due to smaller radii and stronger nuclear pull, it becomes harder to remove valence electrons (higher IE), more energy is released when adding an electron (higher EA), and shared electron attraction increases (higher EN) [150].
- **Trends Down a Group (Top to Bottom):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Increases:* A new principal shell is added with each step down [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Decrease:* Increased shielding of inner shells weakens nuclear hold on the valence shell [144, 150].

#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Modern Periodic Table:** Elements arranged by increasing atomic numbers, reflecting the periodic repetition of properties [151].
- **Core Principle:** Tabular coordinates are determined directly by the electronic configurations of elements [151].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Structural Organization:**
  - *Periods:* 7 horizontal rows [151]. The period number represents the highest filled shell, $n$ [151].
  - *Groups:* 18 vertical columns [137, 151]. Elements in a group share the same number of valence electrons, resulting in similar chemical properties [151].
  - *Blocks:* Classified into four blocks:
    1. *s-block:* Groups 1 and 2 (alkali and alkaline earth metals) [151].
    2. *p-block:* Groups 13 to 18 (noble gases, halogens, non-metals) [151].
    3. *d-block:* Groups 3 to 12 (transition elements) [137, 151].
    4. *f-block:* Lanthanides and Actinides (inner transition elements placed separately) [151].

#### Chemical Equations & Examples
Electronic configuration provides precise coordinate coordinates:
- *Aluminium ($Z=13$):* $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^1$. Since its outermost shell is $n=3$, it is in **Period 3**; its valence electron is in $p$ orbital, placing it in **p-block**; and its total valence electrons are $2+1 = 3$, placing it in **Group 13** ($3 + 10$) [151].
- *Sodium ($Z=11$):* $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$. Outermost shell $n=3$ (**Period 3**), s subshell (**s-block**), has 1 valence electron (**Group 1**) [151].

- **Galvanic Cell Definition:** An electrochemical cell that spontaneously converts chemical energy into electrical energy using spontaneous oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions [137].
- **Cell Construction:**
  1. *Anode Half-cell:* A pure Zinc ($\text{Zn}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Zinc Sulfate ($\text{ZnSO}_4$) solution [147].
  2. *Cathode Half-cell:* A pure Copper ($\text{Cu}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Copper Sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$) solution [147].
  3. *Salt Bridge:* A U-shaped glass tube containing a concentrated gel of potassium chloride ($\text{KCl}$) to maintain electrical neutrality and complete the circuit [147].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
The cell runs on separate half-cell reactions:
- **At Zn Anode (Oxidation half-reaction):** Zinc acts as the negative electrode [138]. Zinc atoms lose two electrons to form Zinc ions which enter the solution [147]:
  $$\text{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \quad (E^\circ = +0.76\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **At Cu Cathode (Reduction half-reaction):** Electrons flow through the wire to the copper electrode [147]. Copper ions in the cathode solution absorb these electrons and deposit as copper [147]:
  $$\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ = +0.34\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **Overall Cell Redox Reaction:**
  $$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = +1.10\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
  This spontaneous reaction yields an electrical potential of $1.1\text{ V}$ [147].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **Galvanic Cell Labeled Diagram:**
  ```
               [ VOLTMETER (e- flow -->) ]
               /                         \
         Zn Anode(-)                     Cu Cathode(+)
         [1M ZnSO4]                      [1M CuSO4]
              \                             /
               \_____[  SALT BRIDGE  ]_____/
                      (K+ and Cl- ions)
  ```
  - *Salt Bridge Mechanism:* Zinc ions accumulate in the anode compartment, and sulfate ions accumulate in the cathode compartment [147]. Inert chloride ions ($\text{Cl}^-$) migrate from the salt bridge to the anode, while potassium ions ($\text{K}^+$) migrate to the cathode, neutralizing charges [147].

(b) Explain the electrolysis of molten sodium chloride in Down's cell. Provide diagrams, chemical equations, and the industrial significance of this process. (4 Marks)
#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Down's Cell Process:** An electrochemical system designed for the commercial-scale extraction of pure metallic sodium through the electrolysis of molten salt ($\text{NaCl}$) [148].
- **Core Principle:** Electric current forces a non-spontaneous redox reaction where sodium ions are reduced to sodium metal and chloride ions are oxidized to chlorine gas [148].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Cell Construction:**
  1. *Anode:* A central graphite rod [148].
  2. *Cathode:* A surrounding circular steel ring [148].
  3. *Steel Wire Gauze:* Separates the chlorine gas from liquid sodium, preventing recombining [148].
  4. *Solvent additive:* Calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$) is added to lower the melting point of $\text{NaCl}$ from $801^\circ\text{C}$ to $600^\circ\text{C}$, saving energy.
- **Product Collection:** Chlorine gas is collected at the anode dome, and molten sodium metal floats to the top of the cathode chamber due to its lower density [148].

#### Chemical Equations & Examples
- **Ionization of NaCl:**
  $$2\text{NaCl(l)} \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} 2\text{Na}^+ + 2\text{Cl}^-$$
- **At Anode (Oxidation half-reaction):**
  $$2\text{Cl}^- \rightarrow \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} + 2e^- \quad [148]$$
- **At Cathode (Reduction half-reaction):**
  $$2\text{Na}^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow 2\text{Na(l)} \quad [148]$$

  $$2\text{NaCl(l)} \rightarrow 2\text{Na(l)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} \quad [148]$$

#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Periodic Properties:** Characteristics of elements showing periodic variations with increasing atomic numbers [149].
  - *Atomic Radius:* Half the distance between the nuclei of two bonded identical atoms [150].
  - *Ionization Energy (IE):* Energy required to remove the outermost electron from a gaseous atom [144].
  - *Electron Affinity (EA):* Energy released when an electron is added to a gaseous atom [150].
  - *Electronegativity (EN):* Relative pull on shared bonding electrons [137, 150].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
- **Trends Across a Period (Left to Right):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Decreases:* Protons are added to the nucleus, increasing the effective nuclear charge, pulling electron shells tighter [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Increase:* Due to smaller radii and stronger nuclear pull, it becomes harder to remove valence electrons (higher IE), more energy is released when adding an electron (higher EA), and shared electron attraction increases (higher EN) [150].
- **Trends Down a Group (Top to Bottom):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Increases:* A new principal shell is added with each step down [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Decrease:* Increased shielding of inner shells weakens nuclear hold on the valence shell [144, 150].

#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Modern Periodic Table:** Elements arranged by increasing atomic numbers, reflecting the periodic repetition of properties [151].
- **Core Principle:** Tabular coordinates are determined directly by the electronic configurations of elements [151].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Structural Organization:**
  - *Periods:* 7 horizontal rows [151]. The period number represents the highest filled shell, $n$ [151].
  - *Groups:* 18 vertical columns [137, 151]. Elements in a group share the same number of valence electrons, resulting in similar chemical properties [151].
  - *Blocks:* Classified into four blocks:
    1. *s-block:* Groups 1 and 2 (alkali and alkaline earth metals) [151].
    2. *p-block:* Groups 13 to 18 (noble gases, halogens, non-metals) [151].
    3. *d-block:* Groups 3 to 12 (transition elements) [137, 151].
    4. *f-block:* Lanthanides and Actinides (inner transition elements placed separately) [151].

#### Chemical Equations & Examples
Electronic configuration provides precise coordinate coordinates:
- *Aluminium ($Z=13$):* $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^1$. Since its outermost shell is $n=3$, it is in **Period 3**; its valence electron is in $p$ orbital, placing it in **p-block**; and its total valence electrons are $2+1 = 3$, placing it in **Group 13** ($3 + 10$) [151].
- *Sodium ($Z=11$):* $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$. Outermost shell $n=3$ (**Period 3**), s subshell (**s-block**), has 1 valence electron (**Group 1**) [151].

- **Galvanic Cell Definition:** An electrochemical cell that spontaneously converts chemical energy into electrical energy using spontaneous oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions [137].

  1. *Anode Half-cell:* A pure Zinc ($\text{Zn}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Zinc Sulfate ($\text{ZnSO}_4$) solution [147].
  2. *Cathode Half-cell:* A pure Copper ($\text{Cu}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Copper Sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$) solution [147].
  3. *Salt Bridge:* A U-shaped glass tube containing a concentrated gel of potassium chloride ($\text{KCl}$) to maintain electrical neutrality and complete the circuit [147].

  $$\text{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \quad (E^\circ = +0.76\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **At Cu Cathode (Reduction half-reaction):** Electrons flow through the wire to the copper electrode [147]. Copper ions in the cathode solution absorb these electrons and deposit as copper [147]:
  $$\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ = +0.34\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **Overall Cell Redox Reaction:**
  $$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = +1.10\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$

              \                             /
               \_____[  SALT BRIDGE  ]_____/

(a) Discuss Electrochemistry in detail. Explain the working and construction of a Galvanic (Voltaic) cell with a complete labeled diagram and half-cell equations. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Galvanic Cell Definition:** An electrochemical cell that spontaneously converts chemical energy into electrical energy using spontaneous oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions [137].
- **Cell Construction:**
  1. *Anode Half-cell:* A pure Zinc ($\text{Zn}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Zinc Sulfate ($\text{ZnSO}_4$) solution [147].
  2. *Cathode Half-cell:* A pure Copper ($\text{Cu}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Copper Sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$) solution [147].
  3. *Salt Bridge:* A U-shaped glass tube containing a concentrated gel of potassium chloride ($\text{KCl}$) to maintain electrical neutrality and complete the circuit [147].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
The cell runs on separate half-cell reactions:
- **At Zn Anode (Oxidation half-reaction):** Zinc acts as the negative electrode [138]. Zinc atoms lose two electrons to form Zinc ions which enter the solution [147]:
  $$\text{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \quad (E^\circ = +0.76\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **At Cu Cathode (Reduction half-reaction):** Electrons flow through the wire to the copper electrode [147]. Copper ions in the cathode solution absorb these electrons and deposit as copper [147]:
  $$\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ = +0.34\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **Overall Cell Redox Reaction:**
  $$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = +1.10\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
  This spontaneous reaction yields an electrical potential of $1.1\text{ V}$ [147].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **Galvanic Cell Labeled Diagram:**
  ```
               [ VOLTMETER (e- flow -->) ]
               /                         \
         Zn Anode(-)                     Cu Cathode(+)
         [1M ZnSO4]                      [1M CuSO4]
              \                             /
               \_____[  SALT BRIDGE  ]_____/
                      (K+ and Cl- ions)
  ```
  - *Salt Bridge Mechanism:* Zinc ions accumulate in the anode compartment, and sulfate ions accumulate in the cathode compartment [147]. Inert chloride ions ($\text{Cl}^-$) migrate from the salt bridge to the anode, while potassium ions ($\text{K}^+$) migrate to the cathode, neutralizing charges [147].

  - *Electron Affinity (EA):* Energy released when an electron is added to a gaseous atom [150].

- **Trends Down a Group (Top to Bottom):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Increases:* A new principal shell is added with each step down [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Decrease:* Increased shielding of inner shells weakens nuclear hold on the valence shell [144, 150].

(a) Discuss Electrochemistry in detail. Explain the working and construction of a Galvanic (Voltaic) cell with a complete labeled diagram and half-cell equations. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Galvanic Cell Definition:** An electrochemical cell that spontaneously converts chemical energy into electrical energy using spontaneous oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions [137].
- **Cell Construction:**
  1. *Anode Half-cell:* A pure Zinc ($\text{Zn}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Zinc Sulfate ($\text{ZnSO}_4$) solution [147].
  2. *Cathode Half-cell:* A pure Copper ($\text{Cu}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Copper Sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$) solution [147].
  3. *Salt Bridge:* A U-shaped glass tube containing a concentrated gel of potassium chloride ($\text{KCl}$) to maintain electrical neutrality and complete the circuit [147].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
The cell runs on separate half-cell reactions:
- **At Zn Anode (Oxidation half-reaction):** Zinc acts as the negative electrode [138]. Zinc atoms lose two electrons to form Zinc ions which enter the solution [147]:
  $$\text{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \quad (E^\circ = +0.76\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **At Cu Cathode (Reduction half-reaction):** Electrons flow through the wire to the copper electrode [147]. Copper ions in the cathode solution absorb these electrons and deposit as copper [147]:
  $$\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ = +0.34\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **Overall Cell Redox Reaction:**
  $$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = +1.10\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
  This spontaneous reaction yields an electrical potential of $1.1\text{ V}$ [147].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **Galvanic Cell Labeled Diagram:**
  ```
               [ VOLTMETER (e- flow -->) ]
               /                         \
         Zn Anode(-)                     Cu Cathode(+)
         [1M ZnSO4]                      [1M CuSO4]
              \                             /
               \_____[  SALT BRIDGE  ]_____/
                      (K+ and Cl- ions)
  ```
  - *Salt Bridge Mechanism:* Zinc ions accumulate in the anode compartment, and sulfate ions accumulate in the cathode compartment [147]. Inert chloride ions ($\text{Cl}^-$) migrate from the salt bridge to the anode, while potassium ions ($\text{K}^+$) migrate to the cathode, neutralizing charges [147].

#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Down's Cell Process:** An electrochemical system designed for the commercial-scale extraction of pure metallic sodium through the electrolysis of molten salt ($\text{NaCl}$) [148].

#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Periodic Properties:** Characteristics of elements showing periodic variations with increasing atomic numbers [149].
  - *Atomic Radius:* Half the distance between the nuclei of two bonded identical atoms [150].
  - *Ionization Energy (IE):* Energy required to remove the outermost electron from a gaseous atom [144].
  - *Electron Affinity (EA):* Energy released when an electron is added to a gaseous atom [150].
  - *Electronegativity (EN):* Relative pull on shared bonding electrons [137, 150].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
- **Trends Across a Period (Left to Right):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Decreases:* Protons are added to the nucleus, increasing the effective nuclear charge, pulling electron shells tighter [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Increase:* Due to smaller radii and stronger nuclear pull, it becomes harder to remove valence electrons (higher IE), more energy is released when adding an electron (higher EA), and shared electron attraction increases (higher EN) [150].
- **Trends Down a Group (Top to Bottom):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Increases:* A new principal shell is added with each step down [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Decrease:* Increased shielding of inner shells weakens nuclear hold on the valence shell [144, 150].

### Question 3 Solution

(a) Discuss Electrochemistry in detail. Explain the working and construction of a Galvanic (Voltaic) cell with a complete labeled diagram and half-cell equations. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Galvanic Cell Definition:** An electrochemical cell that spontaneously converts chemical energy into electrical energy using spontaneous oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions [137].
- **Cell Construction:**
  1. *Anode Half-cell:* A pure Zinc ($\text{Zn}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Zinc Sulfate ($\text{ZnSO}_4$) solution [147].
  2. *Cathode Half-cell:* A pure Copper ($\text{Cu}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Copper Sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$) solution [147].
  3. *Salt Bridge:* A U-shaped glass tube containing a concentrated gel of potassium chloride ($\text{KCl}$) to maintain electrical neutrality and complete the circuit [147].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
The cell runs on separate half-cell reactions:
- **At Zn Anode (Oxidation half-reaction):** Zinc acts as the negative electrode [138]. Zinc atoms lose two electrons to form Zinc ions which enter the solution [147]:
  $$\text{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \quad (E^\circ = +0.76\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **At Cu Cathode (Reduction half-reaction):** Electrons flow through the wire to the copper electrode [147]. Copper ions in the cathode solution absorb these electrons and deposit as copper [147]:
  $$\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ = +0.34\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **Overall Cell Redox Reaction:**
  $$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = +1.10\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
  This spontaneous reaction yields an electrical potential of $1.1\text{ V}$ [147].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **Galvanic Cell Labeled Diagram:**
  ```
               [ VOLTMETER (e- flow -->) ]
               /                         \
         Zn Anode(-)                     Cu Cathode(+)
         [1M ZnSO4]                      [1M CuSO4]
              \                             /
               \_____[  SALT BRIDGE  ]_____/
                      (K+ and Cl- ions)
  ```
  - *Salt Bridge Mechanism:* Zinc ions accumulate in the anode compartment, and sulfate ions accumulate in the cathode compartment [147]. Inert chloride ions ($\text{Cl}^-$) migrate from the salt bridge to the anode, while potassium ions ($\text{K}^+$) migrate to the cathode, neutralizing charges [147].

(b) Explain the electrolysis of molten sodium chloride in Down's cell. Provide diagrams, chemical equations, and the industrial significance of this process. (4 Marks)
#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Down's Cell Process:** An electrochemical system designed for the commercial-scale extraction of pure metallic sodium through the electrolysis of molten salt ($\text{NaCl}$) [148].
- **Core Principle:** Electric current forces a non-spontaneous redox reaction where sodium ions are reduced to sodium metal and chloride ions are oxidized to chlorine gas [148].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Cell Construction:**
  1. *Anode:* A central graphite rod [148].
  2. *Cathode:* A surrounding circular steel ring [148].
  3. *Steel Wire Gauze:* Separates the chlorine gas from liquid sodium, preventing recombining [148].
  4. *Solvent additive:* Calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$) is added to lower the melting point of $\text{NaCl}$ from $801^\circ\text{C}$ to $600^\circ\text{C}$, saving energy.
- **Product Collection:** Chlorine gas is collected at the anode dome, and molten sodium metal floats to the top of the cathode chamber due to its lower density [148].

#### Chemical Equations & Examples
- **Ionization of NaCl:**
  $$2\text{NaCl(l)} \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} 2\text{Na}^+ + 2\text{Cl}^-$$
- **At Anode (Oxidation half-reaction):**
  $$2\text{Cl}^- \rightarrow \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} + 2e^- \quad [148]$$
- **At Cathode (Reduction half-reaction):**
  $$2\text{Na}^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow 2\text{Na(l)} \quad [148]$$
- **Overall Cell Reaction:**
  $$2\text{NaCl(l)} \rightarrow 2\text{Na(l)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} \quad [148]$$

### Question 4 Solution

(a) Define and explain Periodic Properties. Describe the periodic trends of Ionization Energy, Atomic Radius, Electron Affinity, and Electronegativity across periods and down groups. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Periodic Properties:** Characteristics of elements showing periodic variations with increasing atomic numbers [149].
  - *Atomic Radius:* Half the distance between the nuclei of two bonded identical atoms [150].
  - *Ionization Energy (IE):* Energy required to remove the outermost electron from a gaseous atom [144].
  - *Electron Affinity (EA):* Energy released when an electron is added to a gaseous atom [150].
  - *Electronegativity (EN):* Relative pull on shared bonding electrons [137, 150].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
- **Trends Across a Period (Left to Right):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Decreases:* Protons are added to the nucleus, increasing the effective nuclear charge, pulling electron shells tighter [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Increase:* Due to smaller radii and stronger nuclear pull, it becomes harder to remove valence electrons (higher IE), more energy is released when adding an electron (higher EA), and shared electron attraction increases (higher EN) [150].
- **Trends Down a Group (Top to Bottom):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Increases:* A new principal shell is added with each step down [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Decrease:* Increased shielding of inner shells weakens nuclear hold on the valence shell [144, 150].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **Periodic Trends Matrix:**

| Atomic Property | Period Trend (Left-to-Right) | Group Trend (Top-to-Bottom) | Key Driving Force |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Atomic Radius** | Decreases [150]. | Increases [150]. | Increasing nuclear charge vs. addition of new shells [150]. |
| **Ionization Energy**| Increases [150]. | Decreases [144, 150]. | Smaller size vs. increasing shielding effect [144, 150]. |
| **Electron Affinity**| Increases [150]. | Decreases [150]. | Nuclear pull on valence shell vs. added shell distance [150]. |
| **Electronegativity** | Increases [150]. | Decreases [150]. | Nuclear core attraction on bonding pairs [150]. |

(b) Explain the modern periodic table. Discuss periods, groups, blocks (s, p, d, f), and the justification of placing elements based on electronic configurations. (4 Marks)
#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Modern Periodic Table:** Elements arranged by increasing atomic numbers, reflecting the periodic repetition of properties [151].
- **Core Principle:** Tabular coordinates are determined directly by the electronic configurations of elements [151].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Structural Organization:**
  - *Periods:* 7 horizontal rows [151]. The period number represents the highest filled shell, $n$ [151].
  - *Groups:* 18 vertical columns [137, 151]. Elements in a group share the same number of valence electrons, resulting in similar chemical properties [151].
  - *Blocks:* Classified into four blocks:
    1. *s-block:* Groups 1 and 2 (alkali and alkaline earth metals) [151].
    2. *p-block:* Groups 13 to 18 (noble gases, halogens, non-metals) [151].
    3. *d-block:* Groups 3 to 12 (transition elements) [137, 151].
    4. *f-block:* Lanthanides and Actinides (inner transition elements placed separately) [151].

#### Chemical Equations & Examples
Electronic configuration provides precise coordinate coordinates:
- *Aluminium ($Z=13$):* $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^1$. Since its outermost shell is $n=3$, it is in **Period 3**; its valence electron is in $p$ orbital, placing it in **p-block**; and its total valence electrons are $2+1 = 3$, placing it in **Group 13** ($3 + 10$) [151].
- *Sodium ($Z=11$):* $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$. Outermost shell $n=3$ (**Period 3**), s subshell (**s-block**), has 1 valence electron (**Group 1**) [151].

### Question 3 Solution

(a) Discuss Electrochemistry in detail. Explain the working and construction of a Galvanic (Voltaic) cell with a complete labeled diagram and half-cell equations. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Galvanic Cell Definition:** An electrochemical cell that spontaneously converts chemical energy into electrical energy using spontaneous oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions [137].
- **Cell Construction:**
  1. *Anode Half-cell:* A pure Zinc ($\text{Zn}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Zinc Sulfate ($\text{ZnSO}_4$) solution [147].
  2. *Cathode Half-cell:* A pure Copper ($\text{Cu}$) metal rod immersed in a $1\text{ M}$ Copper Sulfate ($\text{CuSO}_4$) solution [147].
  3. *Salt Bridge:* A U-shaped glass tube containing a concentrated gel of potassium chloride ($\text{KCl}$) to maintain electrical neutrality and complete the circuit [147].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
The cell runs on separate half-cell reactions:
- **At Zn Anode (Oxidation half-reaction):** Zinc acts as the negative electrode [138]. Zinc atoms lose two electrons to form Zinc ions which enter the solution [147]:
  $$\text{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \quad (E^\circ = +0.76\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **At Cu Cathode (Reduction half-reaction):** Electrons flow through the wire to the copper electrode [147]. Copper ions in the cathode solution absorb these electrons and deposit as copper [147]:
  $$\text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ = +0.34\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
- **Overall Cell Redox Reaction:**
  $$\text{Zn(s)} + \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)} \quad (E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = +1.10\text{ V}) \quad [147]$$
  This spontaneous reaction yields an electrical potential of $1.1\text{ V}$ [147].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **Galvanic Cell Labeled Diagram:**
  ```
               [ VOLTMETER (e- flow -->) ]
               /                         \
         Zn Anode(-)                     Cu Cathode(+)
         [1M ZnSO4]                      [1M CuSO4]
              \                             /
               \_____[  SALT BRIDGE  ]_____/
                      (K+ and Cl- ions)
  ```
  - *Salt Bridge Mechanism:* Zinc ions accumulate in the anode compartment, and sulfate ions accumulate in the cathode compartment [147]. Inert chloride ions ($\text{Cl}^-$) migrate from the salt bridge to the anode, while potassium ions ($\text{K}^+$) migrate to the cathode, neutralizing charges [147].

(b) Explain the electrolysis of molten sodium chloride in Down's cell. Provide diagrams, chemical equations, and the industrial significance of this process. (4 Marks)
#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Down's Cell Process:** An electrochemical system designed for the commercial-scale extraction of pure metallic sodium through the electrolysis of molten salt ($\text{NaCl}$) [148].
- **Core Principle:** Electric current forces a non-spontaneous redox reaction where sodium ions are reduced to sodium metal and chloride ions are oxidized to chlorine gas [148].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Cell Construction:**
  1. *Anode:* A central graphite rod [148].
  2. *Cathode:* A surrounding circular steel ring [148].
  3. *Steel Wire Gauze:* Separates the chlorine gas from liquid sodium, preventing recombining [148].
  4. *Solvent additive:* Calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$) is added to lower the melting point of $\text{NaCl}$ from $801^\circ\text{C}$ to $600^\circ\text{C}$, saving energy.
- **Product Collection:** Chlorine gas is collected at the anode dome, and molten sodium metal floats to the top of the cathode chamber due to its lower density [148].

#### Chemical Equations & Examples
- **Ionization of NaCl:**
  $$2\text{NaCl(l)} \xrightarrow{\text{heat}} 2\text{Na}^+ + 2\text{Cl}^-$$
- **At Anode (Oxidation half-reaction):**
  $$2\text{Cl}^- \rightarrow \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} + 2e^- \quad [148]$$
- **At Cathode (Reduction half-reaction):**
  $$2\text{Na}^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow 2\text{Na(l)} \quad [148]$$
- **Overall Cell Reaction:**
  $$2\text{NaCl(l)} \rightarrow 2\text{Na(l)} + \text{Cl}_2\text{(g)} \quad [148]$$

(a) Define and explain Periodic Properties. Describe the periodic trends of Ionization Energy, Atomic Radius, Electron Affinity, and Electronegativity across periods and down groups. (6 Marks)
#### Core Concepts & Hybridization Structure
- **Periodic Properties:** Characteristics of elements showing periodic variations with increasing atomic numbers [149].
  - *Atomic Radius:* Half the distance between the nuclei of two bonded identical atoms [150].
  - *Ionization Energy (IE):* Energy required to remove the outermost electron from a gaseous atom [144].
  - *Electron Affinity (EA):* Energy released when an electron is added to a gaseous atom [150].
  - *Electronegativity (EN):* Relative pull on shared bonding electrons [137, 150].

#### Detailed Analysis & Properties
- **Trends Across a Period (Left to Right):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Decreases:* Protons are added to the nucleus, increasing the effective nuclear charge, pulling electron shells tighter [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Increase:* Due to smaller radii and stronger nuclear pull, it becomes harder to remove valence electrons (higher IE), more energy is released when adding an electron (higher EA), and shared electron attraction increases (higher EN) [150].
- **Trends Down a Group (Top to Bottom):**
  1. *Atomic Radius Increases:* A new principal shell is added with each step down [150].
  2. *IE, EA, and EN Decrease:* Increased shielding of inner shells weakens nuclear hold on the valence shell [144, 150].

#### Scientific Evidence & Diagram Description
- **Periodic Trends Matrix:**

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Ionization Energy**| Increases [150]. | Decreases [144, 150]. | Smaller size vs. increasing shielding effect [144, 150]. |
| **Electron Affinity**| Increases [150]. | Decreases [150]. | Nuclear pull on valence shell vs. added shell distance [150]. |
| **Electronegativity** | Increases [150]. | Decreases [150]. | Nuclear core attraction on bonding pairs [150]. |

(b) Explain the modern periodic table. Discuss periods, groups, blocks (s, p, d, f), and the justification of placing elements based on electronic configurations. (4 Marks)
#### Core Principles & Definition
- **Modern Periodic Table:** Elements arranged by increasing atomic numbers, reflecting the periodic repetition of properties [151].
- **Core Principle:** Tabular coordinates are determined directly by the electronic configurations of elements [151].

#### Technical Analysis & Structural Features
- **Structural Organization:**
  - *Periods:* 7 horizontal rows [151]. The period number represents the highest filled shell, $n$ [151].
  - *Groups:* 18 vertical columns [137, 151]. Elements in a group share the same number of valence electrons, resulting in similar chemical properties [151].
  - *Blocks:* Classified into four blocks:
    1. *s-block:* Groups 1 and 2 (alkali and alkaline earth metals) [151].
    2. *p-block:* Groups 13 to 18 (noble gases, halogens, non-metals) [151].
    3. *d-block:* Groups 3 to 12 (transition elements) [137, 151].
    4. *f-block:* Lanthanides and Actinides (inner transition elements placed separately) [151].

#### Chemical Equations & Examples
Electronic configuration provides precise coordinate coordinates:
- *Aluminium ($Z=13$):* $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^1$. Since its outermost shell is $n=3$, it is in **Period 3**; its valence electron is in $p$ orbital, placing it in **p-block**; and its total valence electrons are $2+1 = 3$, placing it in **Group 13** ($3 + 10$) [151].
- *Sodium ($Z=11$):* $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^1$. Outermost shell $n=3$ (**Period 3**), s subshell (**s-block**), has 1 valence electron (**Group 1**) [151].
