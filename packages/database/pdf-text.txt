Section B Model Answers
Question 2 (part 1) Solution:
Option A: Define Organic Chemistry and differentiate it from Inorganic Chemistry.
Model  Answer  A:  Organic  Chemistry  deals  with  covalent  carbon  compounds.  Inorganic  Chemistry  deals  with  all  other
elements and compounds [1].
Option B: Define Biochemistry and state its primary focus in living organisms.
Model Answer B: Biochemistry deals with the chemical substances and physical/chemical changes in living organisms [1].

Question 2 (part 2) Solution:
Option A: What is Green Chemistry, and why is it important for the environment?
Model  Answer  A:  Green  Chemistry  focuses  on  designing  chemical  products  and  processes  that  reduce  or  eliminate
hazardous substances [1].
Option B: Describe Environmental Chemistry and its role in combating pollution.
Model Answer B: Environmental Chemistry deals with chemical and toxic substances in the environment and their biological
impacts [1].
Question 2 (part 3) Solution:
Option A: Compare the macroscopic properties of gases and solids regarding density and compressibility.
Model Answer A: Gases have low density and high compressibility; solids have high density and are incompressible [17].
Option B: Explain why gases are highly compressible while solids are virtually incompressible.
Model  Answer  B:  Gases  have  large  empty  spaces  between  molecules,  allowing  compression;  solid  molecules  are  tightly
packed [17].
Question 2 (part 4) Solution:
Option A: Define an Element and describe how it differs from a Compound.
Model  Answer  A:  An  element  consists  of  only  one  type  of  atom.  A  compound  consists  of  different  elements  chemically
combined [18].
Option B: Define a Mixture and distinguish between Homogeneous and Heterogeneous mixtures.
Model  Answer  B:  A  mixture  is  physically  combined  and  can  be  homogeneous  (uniform,  e.g.,  solution)  or  heterogeneous
(non-uniform, e.g., suspension) [19].
Question 2 (part 5) Solution:
Option A: What are Allotropes? Name three allotropic forms of carbon.
Model  Answer  A:  Allotropes  are  different  physical  forms  of  the  same  element.  Carbon  allotropes:  Diamond,  Graphite,
Buckyballs [18].
Option B: Describe the structure and electrical conductivity of Graphite.
Model Answer B: Graphite has 2D hexagonal layers with free delocalized electrons, making it a good electrical conductor
[18].
Question 2 (part 6) Solution:
Option A: Explain why Diamond is extremely hard and acts as an electrical insulator.
Model Answer A: Diamond has a rigid 3D tetrahedral network of covalently bonded carbon with no free electrons, making it
hard and an insulator [18, 19].
Option B: Describe Buckyballs (C-60) and state their physical properties and molecular shape.
Model  Answer  B:  Buckyballs  are  football-shaped  hollow  cages  of  60  carbon  atoms,  containing  pentagons  and  hexagons
[19].
Question 2 (part 7) Solution:
Option A: Define a Solution and explain the terms Solute and Solvent with examples.
Model  Answer  A:  A  solution  is  a  homogeneous  mixture.  Solute  is  dissolved  (e.g.,  salt);  solvent  does  the  dissolving  (e.g.,
water) [19].
Option B: What is an Aqueous Solution? Give two common laboratory examples.
Model Answer B: An aqueous solution uses water as the solvent. Examples: NaCl in water, CuSO4 in water [20].

Question 2 (part 8) Solution:
Option A: Differentiate between Saturated and Unsaturated solutions based on solute concentration.
Model Answer A: Saturated has max solute at that temperature; unsaturated has less than saturated capacity [21].
Option B: What is a Supersaturated Solution? How can it be prepared in the laboratory?
Model  Answer  B:  Supersaturated  contains  more  solute  than  saturated.  Prepared  by  heating  saturated  solution,  adding
solute, and cooling slowly [21].
Question 2 (part 9) Solution:
Option A: State the effect of temperature on the solubility of NaCl and Ca(OH)2.
Model  Answer  A:  NaCl  solubility  increases  slightly  with  temperature;  Ca(OH)2  solubility  decreases  with  increasing
temperature [22].
Option B: Describe how temperature affects the solubility of NH4Cl compared to Ce2(SO4)3.
Model Answer B: NH4Cl solubility increases sharply with temperature, while Ce2(SO4)3 decreases [22].
Question 2 (part 10) Solution:
Option A: Define Colloids and explain the Tyndall Effect with a real-life example.
Model Answer A: Colloids have intermediate particle size and scatter light (Tyndall Effect), e.g., light beam through milk [24].
Option B: What is a Suspension? Explain why it is heterogeneous and unstable.
Model  Answer  B:  A  suspension  has  large  particles  (>1000  nm)  that  do  not  dissolve  and  settle  down  upon  standing,  e.g.,
muddy water [24].
Question 2 (part 11) Solution:
Option A: Differentiate between 'Science', 'Technology', and 'Engineering'.
Model  Answer  A:  Science  seeks  to  understand  nature.  Technology  applies  science.  Engineering  designs  and  builds
systems [9, 10].
Option B: Provide a real-life example of how Science, Technology, and Engineering collaborate.
Model  Answer  B:  Harnessing  solar  energy:  Science  studies  photovoltaic  effect,  Technology  creates  solar  panels,
Engineering constructs solar farms [11].
Question 2 (part 12) Solution:
Option A: How does Analytical Chemistry assist forensic scientists in criminal investigations?
Model  Answer  A:  Forensic  chemistry  uses  analytical  tools  to  identify  physical  traces,  drugs,  toxins,  and  paint  at  crime
scenes [1].
Option B: Explain the role of Medicinal Chemistry in modern healthcare and pharmacology.
Model Answer B: Medicinal chemistry designs and synthesizes drugs that interact with biological targets to treat diseases
[1].
Question 2 (part 13) Solution:
Option A: Explain the concept of Plasma as the fourth state of matter.
Model Answer A: Plasma is highly ionized gas consisting of free electrons, positive ions, and excited neutral atoms [16, 17].
Option B: What is a Bose-Einstein Condensate, and under what conditions does it exist?
Model Answer B: BEC is a state of matter formed when separate atoms are cooled to temperatures very close to absolute
zero [16].
Question 2 (part 14) Solution:
Option A: What is an alloy? Give the composition and uses of Brass.
Model  Answer  A:  An  alloy  is  a  solid  solution  of  metals.  Brass  is  made  of  copper  and  zinc,  used  in  decorative  items  and
plumbing [20].
Option B: Differentiate between concentrated and dilute solutions.
Model Answer B: Concentrated solution contains relatively large solute; dilute contains a relatively small amount [22].

Section C Model Answers
Question 3 Solution:
Option  A:  Analyze  the  three  main  allotropes  of  carbon:  Diamond,  Graphite,  and  Buckyballs.  Differentiate  them  based  on
hybridization/structure, conductivity, and hardness.
Detailed Model Answer A: Diamond has tetrahedral structure, no free electrons (insulator), and is extremely hard. Graphite
has planar hexagonal sheets, free electrons (conductor), and is soft/slippery. Buckyballs have hollow spherical cages [18,
19].
Option  B:  Explain  the  classification  of  matter.  Draw  the  complete  hierarchy  diagram  showing  pure  substances,  mixtures,
elements, compounds, colloids, and suspensions.
Detailed Model Answer B: Matter divides into Pure Substances (Elements & Compounds) and Mixtures (Homogeneous like
Solutions, Heterogeneous like Colloids and Suspensions) [19].
Question 4 Solution:
Option A: Describe the preparation and testing of Saturated, Unsaturated, and Supersaturated solutions using sugar and
water as an experimental example.
Detailed Model Answer A: Add sugar to 100g water until it stops dissolving (saturated). Add more water (unsaturated). Heat
saturated solution, add more sugar, and cool slowly (supersaturated) [21].
Option B: Define Solubility. Analyze the effect of temperature on solubility curves, citing examples of solute types whose
solubility increases, decreases, or remains constant.
Detailed Model Answer B: Solubility is max solute in 100g solvent. KNO3/NH4Cl increase with temperature. Ca(OH)2/SO2
gas decrease with temperature. NaCl remains nearly constant [22, 23].
Question 5 Solution:
Option A: Compare Solutions, Colloids, and Suspensions in a comprehensive matrix based on particle size, homogeneity,
Tyndall effect, filtration, and settling behavior.
Detailed  Model  Answer  A:  Solutions  (<1nm,  homogeneous,  no  Tyndall,  passes  filter,  doesn't  settle).  Colloids  (1-1000nm,
heterogeneous-looking  homogeneous,  Tyndall,  passes  filter,  doesn't  settle).  Suspensions  (>1000nm,  heterogeneous,
Tyndall, retained, settles) [24].
Option  B:  Explain  with  examples  how  science,  technology,  and  engineering  are  interconnected.  Discuss  three  daily  life
applications of chemistry.
Detailed  Model  Answer  B:  Science  discovers  laws;  engineering  designs  systems;  technology  manufactures.  Chemistry
applications: medicines (organic), batteries (inorganic), forensic tests (analytical) [8, 9, 10].
Question 6 Solution:
Option A: Formulate essential questions for physical, organic, inorganic, and biochemistry. Explain how these fields solve
global challenges like energy and health.
Detailed  Model  Answer  A:  Physical:  How  do  bonds  form?  Organic:  Why  is  carbon  the  backbone?  Inorganic:  How  does
periodic  table  organize  elements?  Biochemistry:  How  do  proteins  function?  These  address  energy  (batteries)  and  health
(drugs) [7, 8].
Option B: Discuss the characteristics of water-filtration and solar-energy systems, detailing the role of chemical principles in
their design.
Detailed  Model  Answer  B:  Solar-energy  uses  semiconductor  chemistry.  Water  filtration  uses  chemical  coagulation,
sedimentation, and active carbon adsorption to remove contaminants [11].
